GreenWatch Orbit

Compare satellite images across dates and see where forest may be disappearing.

NASA GIBS · MODIS and VIIRS imagery

Move both maps together, then press “Detect change in view”.

Where pressure is highest

Start with a region known for forest conversion, or drag the maps anywhere in the world. Reference list The notes describe common drivers, not findings from the satellite images.

    How the comparison works

    1. Pick two dates

    • The right map shows the “after” date. The left shows the same place one or more years earlier.
    • Using the same time of year keeps seasons from looking like loss.

    2. Flag change

    • The page measures how green each pixel is on both dates and skips clouds and missing data.
    • Red marks a drop in greenness. Cyan marks a rise. Orange dots are active fires from VIIRS.

    3. Check, then report

    • Open the flagged place in higher-resolution imagery before you act on it.
    • “Create report” saves the images, numbers, method, and limits as one file.

    Verified NASA data Imagery is NASA MODIS Terra and VIIRS from GIBS, at roughly 250 m detail. The change flags are our own screening calculation, so they are indicators only. Screening result

    Read the results with care

    What looks like loss but isn’t

    • Harvested or fallow crops, dry-season browning, flooding, and burn scars from natural fire.
    • Cloud shadow and haze that slip past the cloud filter.

    What it can miss

    • Selective logging and small clearings under about 6 hectares.
    • Loss under cloud on either date.

    Confirm with

    • Global Forest Watch, for published tree-cover loss alerts
    • NASA Worldview, for more layers and dates
    • NASA Earthdata Search and USGS EarthExplorer, for Landsat scenes
