AgroOrbit is the farming side of EcoOrbit.

EcoOrbit tracks satellites and debris in low Earth orbit. AgroOrbit points that same orbit knowledge at the ground. It collects satellite images of farmland, reads them with AI, and sends each farmer short advice they can act on the same day.

A farmer does not need to understand satellites, indexes or maps. The message says which plot, what is wrong, and what to do about it. Cooperatives, officers and banks see the same facts on one map.

Farmers
Find the corner of a field that needs water or attention without walking all of it.
Cooperatives
Follow hundreds of members' fields on one map and plan shared pumps and harvest crews.
Extension officers
Visit the fields that need help first, instead of covering every village in turn.
Banks and insurers
Check crop loss claims against satellite evidence, with the farmer's consent.
Local government and NGOs
Watch for floods, drought and salty soil across a district during the season.

From a satellite pass to a message on your phone

Four steps, repeated every time a satellite flies over your district.

  1. 1

    A satellite passes over

    Optical satellites photograph the fields in daylight. Radar satellites see through cloud, which matters in the monsoon. EcoOrbit's orbit simulator tells AgroOrbit when the next pass over each district will happen.

    You get: a fresh image of every field, every few days.

  2. 2

    AI reads each plot

    The model measures how green the crop is, how wet the soil is and where water is standing. It compares each plot with its own past, its neighbours and the crop calendar for that season.

    You get: a health, moisture and risk reading for every plot.

  3. 3

    You get a short message

    Alerts arrive in Bangla or English, as an app notification or an SMS for basic phones. Each one names the plot, the problem and the first thing to do.

    You get: advice such as "Irrigate the south-west corner within two days."

  4. 4

    Your actions become a record

    You mark what you did: irrigated, drained, replanted. Over the season these entries, checked against later satellite images, build a farm record that banks, insurers and carbon programs can trust.

    You get: a season history you can share when you choose to.

What AgroOrbit does for a farm

Six tools that share the same map and the same plot boundaries.

Crop health map

A greenness reading for every plot, compared with last week and with healthy fields nearby. Weak patches show up before they are visible from the path.

Soil moisture and irrigation advice

Tells you which plots to water and which to leave alone, so pumps run only when the crop needs them and diesel or electricity is not wasted.

Flood and drought early warning

Radar shows standing water and rising rivers through cloud. Alerts reach you with time to drain, harvest early or move seed and equipment.

Yield forecast

An estimate of harvest size per plot, updated as the season goes. It helps you book labour and storage, and talk to buyers with real numbers.

Stress watch

Flags patches that are weaker than their surroundings in a way water does not explain. It does not diagnose, it sends you to the exact spot to look for pests, disease or missing nutrients.

Farm records and carbon credits

Logs your irrigation and field practice, confirms it from space, and prepares the evidence a carbon program needs to pay you for lower emissions.

What AgroOrbit watches

    Places that need different watching

    Haor basin (Sunamganj, Sylhet, Kishoreganj) Early flash floods can hit just before the Boro harvest, so flood alerts come first here.
    Coastal belt (Khulna, Satkhira, Bagerhat) Salty soil and water, and cyclone damage, decide what can be planted.
    Barind Tract (Rajshahi, Naogaon, Chapainawabganj) Dry spells and falling groundwater make irrigation timing the main question.
    Char lands and river banks Fields change shape each year, so plot boundaries are redrawn from fresh images.

    Turn better water use into carbon records

    Flooded rice paddies release methane, a strong greenhouse gas. Letting the field dry out briefly between irrigations, called alternate wetting and drying, cuts that methane and also saves pumping cost.

    AgroOrbit shows from satellite images when each plot was flooded and when it was dry. That gives carbon programs evidence they can check, and EcoOrbit's offset platform is where the verified result can be listed. Try the estimator to see the scale for a farm.

    Where the information comes from

    AgroOrbit combines several sources, because no single one works every day.

    Source What it tells us How often
    Optical satellites How green and dense the crop is, and its growth stage. Detail of about 10 metres. About every 5 days when the sky is clear
    Radar satellites Standing water, flood extent and soil wetness. Works through cloud, day or night. About every 6 to 12 days
    Weather data Rainfall, heat, cold spells and short-range forecasts for each plot. Several times a day

    Questions farmers and partners ask

    Do I need a smartphone?

    No. Alerts also arrive as short SMS messages in Bangla or English, and a cooperative officer can go through the map with you.

    Can it see through monsoon clouds?

    Radar images pass through cloud, so flood and soil wetness readings keep coming. Crop greenness readings from optical images pause under heavy cloud and resume at the next clear pass.

    How small a field can it read?

    The images have about 10 metre detail, so a plot of around 0.1 hectare gets roughly ten readings. Smaller plots are read together with the land around them.

    How accurate is the advice?

    AgroOrbit gives guidance, not guarantees. It points you to the spot worth checking, and a walk through the field confirms it. Your feedback improves the model each season.

    Who owns my field data?

    You do. Plot boundaries and records stay yours, and they are shared with a bank, insurer or carbon program only when you allow it.
