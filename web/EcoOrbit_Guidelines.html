EcoOrbit Brand Guidelines

EcoOrbit is an AI-driven orbit carbon and debris offset platform built for NASA Space Apps 2026. Everyone who builds, shares or writes about EcoOrbit (the 3D globe, the Orbit Sustainability Score, Collision AI and the Junior Space Explorers Hub) should present a consistent and trustworthy image, so these guidelines explain how to use our logo, colors, fonts, badges and patterns in a predictable way.

These guidelines cover logo usage, color, typography, shapes and symbols, badges and patterns for both screen and print.

Logos

The EcoOrbit logo can be considered a "responsive" logo: it takes one of several forms depending on the space and resolution available.

The Default Logo is the preferred option. It pairs the orbit mark with the EcoOrbit wordmark and the "NASA Space Apps 2026" tagline, and is designed to look best in the widest range of applications.

Colors

The color palette favors deep space blues with vivid neon accents that evoke the depths of orbit, the glow of telemetry and the health of our planet. Neon Emerald signals positive, sustainable results and Alert Pink is reserved for warnings and debris collisions. When using color combinations, make sure the contrast ratio meets or exceeds 4.5:1 for body text (3:1 for large text). Click any swatch to copy its HEX code.

When desired, a gradient effect can be used as the background of designs and buttons. The gradient should be linear and extend from the top-left Neon Cyan, through Sky Blue, toward the bottom-right Orbit Navy and Deep Space, ideally at a 135° angle. The transition should take place across the entirety of the field, and not abruptly. If necessary, manually ease the gradient so that the transition between colors does not appear sudden or otherwise stand out in any way.

Every object in orbit has a carbon cost. EcoOrbit measures it, predicts collisions with AI, and turns good behavior into carbon credits.

Typography color: Bodies of text should only be placed against Deep Space, Orbit Navy, or on white, using White, Neon Cyan, Sky Blue or Slate Text. Avoid using Alert Pink as a background color. Special combinations of text colors are available to differentiate content sections. Use Neon Emerald sparingly, ideally to make a positive status or score stand out from other text.

Shapes, Symbols, Badges

Shapes and symbols should be used only when their meaning is reasonably unambiguous. Otherwise, prefer text labels over symbols. Suitable icon libraries include Font Awesome (used across EcoOrbit) or Feather styles.

Badges are depicted as illustrated icons representing the ten EcoOrbit modules and achievements. Badges can be placed into a circular holding shape to create a "badge" if desired.

Patterns

A number of patterns have been created to add visual interest to EcoOrbit materials. They represent abstractions of things such as orbits, coordinates, waves, cells, code and geometry, and help lend a scientific or technological look and feel wherever they are used.

These patterns should be screened back to roughly 20% opacity over a brand color, preferably one in the blue range.
