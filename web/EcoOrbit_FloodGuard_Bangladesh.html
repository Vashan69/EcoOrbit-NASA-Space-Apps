What the map shows

Rainfall comes from NASA’s Global Precipitation Measurement (GPM) IMERG product, drawn through NASA Earthdata’s GIBS service. Turn it on over the true-colour image to see storms and swollen, silty rivers together. Verified NASA data

Rain already fallen

Select a district to see its last 16 days of rainfall from NASA POWER, which is built from satellite and model data. The newest days can be missing while NASA processes them. Verified NASA data

Communities and infrastructure

Markers show the districts most often hit by river and coastal flooding. Zoom to district level and press “Show hospitals in view” to load hospitals mapped by volunteers on OpenStreetMap. Prepare and evacuate

Simple steps that matter most. Local authorities decide where shelters are and when to leave, so follow their instructions first.

Before the flood

  • Agree on a meeting point and a route to higher ground or the nearest shelter.
  • Pack drinking water, dry food, medicine, a torch, a charged phone, and a power bank.
  • Keep ID, land papers, and certificates in a sealed plastic bag.
  • Move livestock, seed, and tools to raised ground.

When water rises

  • Leave early when the warning comes. Do not wait for the water to reach the door.
  • Switch off power and gas. Never walk or drive through moving water.
  • Take children, older people, and people with disabilities first.
  • Tell a neighbour or relative where you are going.

After the flood

  • Drink only boiled or treated water. Tube wells may be contaminated.
  • Stay away from fallen power lines and damaged buildings.
  • Photograph damage before cleaning, for relief and insurance claims.
  • Watch for diarrhoea, fever, and skin infections, and seek care early.
